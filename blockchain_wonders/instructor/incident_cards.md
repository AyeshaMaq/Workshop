# Governance Crisis Challenge: incident cards

Print and cut. One card per team. In the notebook: `run_incident(N)`.

---

## Incident 1: The uninvited agent

A bot nobody has heard of starts submitting grant approvals.

**Think:** Who is allowed to take part, and how does the system know?

---

## Incident 2: The silent model swap

A registered agent is retrained overnight. Nobody was told.

**Think:** How do we know which model made a decision?

---

## Incident 3: The edited decision

A decision record says APPROVE, but the model actually said REJECT.

**Think:** If the AI is deterministic, what can validators do that they could not do otherwise?

---

## Incident 4: The disagreement

Two agents give opposite answers for the same application.

**Think:** Should a machine break the tie, or a person?

---

## Incident 5: The typo

An application arrives with a project score of 820 out of 100.

**Think:** The AI was "correct" for the data it saw. Who checks the data?

---

## Incident 6: The big cheque

The largest grant of the year is approved fully automatically.

**Think:** Where should automation stop and human judgement begin?

---

## Incident 7: The eager validator

A case is confirmed seconds after submission, on one person's votes.

**Think:** How many people must agree before something counts as agreed?

---

## Incident 8: The rewritten past

An old REJECT on the ledger now reads APPROVE.

**Think:** A blockchain makes tampering evident. Evident to whom, and when?

---

## Team report (two minutes)

1. Which rule stopped your incident?
2. Was it stopped by the blockchain, by deterministic AI, by governance rules, or by a human?
3. Name one rule you added that has a cost. What is the cost?
4. Name one thing this system still could not stop.
